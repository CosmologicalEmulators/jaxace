    Neutrino thermal prescriptions¶

    The analytic background treats three explicit physical neutrino masses with zero-chemical-potential Fermi–Dirac distributions, plus a massless radiation remainder. Changing Neff is not enough to specify how that massive-neutrino sector changes: jaxace provides two distinct thermal prescriptions.

    This page describes the analytic model, not the frozen neural-emulator training labels. Neural emulators still take total neutrino mass with the splitting used in their training data. See Cosmology API for the callable API and the separate growth approximations.

    For example, compare expansion at the same physical parameters:

    import jax
jax.config.update("jax_enable_x64", True)
import jax.numpy as jnp
import jaxace

z = jnp.array([0.0, 1.0, 10.0])
common = dict(ln10As=3.044, ns=0.965, h=0.67, omega_b=0.0224, omega_c=0.12,
              m_nu=(0.01, 0.02, 0.03), Neff=4.0)
temperature = jaxace.w0waCDMCosmology(
    **common, neutrino_prescription="temperature")
radiation = jaxace.w0waCDMCosmology(
    **common, neutrino_prescription="radiation")
E_temperature = temperature.E_z(z)
E_radiation = radiation.E_z(z)
assert jnp.allclose(E_temperature[0], 1.0)
assert jnp.allclose(E_radiation[0], 1.0)
print(jnp.column_stack((z, E_temperature, E_radiation)))

    Energy density and the meaning of Neff¶

    Let \(a=1/(1+z)\), with photon temperature \(T_\gamma(a)=T_{\gamma,0}/a\) and \(T_{\gamma,0}=2.7255\,\mathrm{K}\). All three species share the temperature ratio \(r=T_\nu/T_\gamma\) within each prescription; their masses may differ. Here \(m_i\) denotes the rest energy in eV (\(c=1\)), and \(k_{\mathrm{B}}\) is expressed in eV/K. Define

    \[ F(y)=\int_0^\infty \frac{q^2\sqrt{q^2+y^2}}{e^q+1}\,\mathrm{d}q, \qquad y_i(a)=\frac{m_i a}{k_{\mathrm{B}}rT_{\gamma,0}}, \qquad F(0)=\frac{7\pi^4}{120}. \]

    With unit degeneracy per species in CLASS's deg_ncdm convention, including the usual neutrino/antineutrino pair, the total neutrino-sector density is

    \[ \frac{\rho_\nu(a)}{\rho_\gamma(a)} =\frac{15r^4}{\pi^4}\sum_{i=1}^{3} F\!\left(y_i(a)\right) +\frac{7}{8}r_{\mathrm{inst}}^4 N_{\mathrm{ur}}, \qquad r_{\mathrm{inst}}=\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{1/3}. \]

    Here \(N_{\mathrm{ur}}\) is the effective count of the massless remainder, not the total Neff. It can be fractional. Throughout this page and the figure, \(\rho_\nu\) includes both the three Fermi–Dirac species and that remainder, but not photons.

    \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) specifies the density in the relativistic limit:

    \[ \left.\frac{\rho_\nu}{\rho_\gamma}\right|_{y_i\to0} =\frac{7}{8}r_{\mathrm{inst}}^4 N_{\mathrm{eff}}, \qquad N_{\mathrm{eff}}=3\left(\frac{r}{r_{\mathrm{inst}}}\right)^4+N_{\mathrm{ur}}. \]

    It is not a formula for the late-time energy density once neutrinos become nonrelativistic. The mass-dependent integral must still be evaluated. jaxace tabulates the integral at import and uses lookup interpolation, extended by a moment series at large arguments, at runtime. It does not perform fresh momentum quadrature at every background call.

    Common reference convention¶

    Both prescriptions share the reference

    \[ N_{\mathrm{eff,ref}}=3.044,\qquad r_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.71611, \qquad N_{\mathrm{ur,ref}} =3.044-3\left(\frac{0.71611}{r_{\mathrm{inst}}}\right)^4 \simeq 0.00439512. \]

    The temperatures use the CLASS anchor, and the remainder makes the total relativistic density correspond to exactly 3.044. This is an effective thermal model, not an evolution of neutrino decoupling or a nonthermal distribution. The photon temperature and individual physical masses are held fixed when Neff changes.

    Temperature rescaling¶

    The default neutrino_prescription="temperature" sets

    \[ s=\frac{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}{N_{\mathrm{eff,ref}}},\qquad r=r_{\mathrm{ref}}s^{1/4},\qquad N_{\mathrm{ur}}=N_{\mathrm{ur,ref}}s. \]

    Both terms in the relativistic density therefore scale by \(s\). Input validation requires finite \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}>0\). Increasing it heats the three species; decreasing it cools them. In particular,

    \[ n_i(a)\propto r^3a^{-3},\qquad \frac{n_i}{n_{i,\mathrm{ref}}}=s^{3/4},\qquad \rho_{\nu,i}\simeq m_i n_i\quad\text{when }y_i\gg1. \]

    Thus varying Neff also changes the late-time massive-neutrino density at fixed masses, and shifts when each species becomes nonrelativistic. At \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=4\), temperatures increase by about 7.07% and massive-species number densities by about 22.73% relative to the reference.

    Input acceptance is not an accuracy guarantee over all masses, temperatures and scale factors. The saved CLASS tests sample \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=2,3.044,5\) for this prescription; the figure adds a case at 4. Neither those tests nor the positivity check certify arbitrary extreme inputs.

    Fixed temperatures, variable massless radiation¶

    With neutrino_prescription="radiation", the three species keep their reference temperatures and only the massless remainder changes:

    \[ r=r_{\mathrm{ref}},\qquad N_{\mathrm{ur}}=N_{\mathrm{eff}} -3\left(\frac{r_{\mathrm{ref}}}{r_{\mathrm{inst}}}\right)^4. \]

    Their number densities and nonrelativistic transitions are unchanged. The additional massless density redshifts as \(a^{-4}\). Nonnegative massless density requires

    \[ N_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge 3\left(\frac{r_{\mathrm{ref}}}{r_{\mathrm{inst}}}\right)^4 \simeq 3.03960488. \]

    For example, Neff=2 cannot represent this prescription. jaxace returns NaN for invalid numerical domains, including under JIT, rather than silently switching to temperature rescaling. Incorrect mass shapes and unknown static prescription names raise ValueError.

    Both prescriptions coincide at \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=3.044\). At other allowed values they have the same relativistic-limit energy density but generally different massive-neutrino backgrounds. If all three masses are exactly zero, their total neutrino-sector density remains identical at every scale factor for the same Neff.

    Expansion rate and closure¶

    Neutrinos enter the background through their full energy density:

    \[ E^2(a)=\frac{H^2(a)}{H_0^2} =\Omega_{\mathrm{cb},0}a^{-3}+\Omega_{\gamma,0}a^{-4} +\frac{\rho_\nu(a)}{\rho_{\mathrm{crit},0}} +\Omega_{\mathrm{k},0}a^{-2} +\Omega_{\mathrm{DE},0}f_{\mathrm{DE}}(a), \]
    \[ f_{\mathrm{DE}}(a)=a^{-3(1+w_0+w_a)}e^{3w_a(a-1)},\qquad \Omega_{\mathrm{DE},0}=1-\Omega_{\mathrm{cb},0}-\Omega_{\gamma,0} -\frac{\rho_\nu(1)}{\rho_{\mathrm{crit},0}}-\Omega_{\mathrm{k},0}. \]

    \(\Omega_{\mathrm{cb},0}=\Omega_{\mathrm{c},0}+\Omega_{\mathrm{b},0}\) excludes neutrinos. At fixed \(H_0\), changing the neutrino sector changes the dark-energy density required by closure. Consequently all curves have the same \(H(0)=H_0\); the comparison does not also hold \(\Omega_{\mathrm{DE},0}\) fixed.

    jaxace compared with matched CLASS calculations¶

    Vector SVG · Image storage and caption

    The solid curves are jaxace only; circles are CLASS v3.3.4. Blue denotes temperature rescaling, orange denotes the fixed-temperature radiation prescription. The upper panels show percentage changes relative to a common CLASS reference at \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=3.044\), with the same masses, \(H_0\), \(\omega_{\mathrm{b}}\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{c}}\). The lower panels show jaxace/CLASS fractional residuals, multiplied by the factors printed on the axes. Those tiny residuals are numerical differences, not the physical difference between the two prescriptions.

    For this example, \(m_\nu=(0.01,0.02,0.03)\,\mathrm{eV}\), \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=4\), \(h=0.67\), \(\omega_{\mathrm{b}}=0.0224\), \(\omega_{\mathrm{c}}=0.12\), \(\Omega_{\mathrm{k},0}=0\) and \(w=-1\). The illustrative mass split is not an imposed oscillation hierarchy. The maximum absolute fractional residuals on the plotted grid are below \(3\times10^{-8}\) for density and \(2\times10^{-9}\) for expansion. These are this configuration's diagnostics, not a bound over all parameters or a validation of scale-dependent perturbation growth.

    Matching CLASS, rather than comparing unmatched defaults¶

    For each prescription CLASS is configured with the same m_ncdm, T_ncdm, deg_ncdm=1 per massive species and N_ur specified above. Its T_ncdm input is the ratio to the photon temperature, not a temperature in kelvin.

    CLASS input at \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=4\) Temperature rescaling Fixed-temperature radiation
    N_ncdm 3 3
    m_ncdm [eV] 0.01,0.02,0.03 0.01,0.02,0.03
    T_ncdm (each species) 0.766714319146159 0.71611
    N_ur 0.00577545013072 0.96039511754948

    Both calculations use T_cmb=2.7255, YHe=0.245, tol_ncdm_bg=1e-12, tol_background_integration=1e-12 and background_integration_stepsize=0.01. Only CLASS's background module is needed. The 321 plotted samples per prescription are selected from its native background rows over \(0\le z\lesssim10^5\). Density residuals compare directly to those rows; only the common reference in the upper panels is interpolated in \(\ln a\) and \(\ln\rho_\nu\)/\(\ln H\).

    When matching a case containing an exactly zero mass, transfer that species to CLASS's N_ur with contribution \((r/r_{\mathrm{inst}})^4\) instead of passing a zero m_ncdm; keep the same total relativistic density. The figure uses three positive masses, so that conversion is not needed here.

    Reproduce this figure¶

    The shared figure sources and instructions include the actual CLASS generator, both implementation evaluators, the plotting script, provenance and all six frozen text arrays. Select jaxace when plotting to reproduce this page's figure. Replot from the saved arrays without CLASS or Julia, or regenerate the CLASS and jaxace arrays with reference.py. The Julia evaluator is needed only for the Julia figure.
